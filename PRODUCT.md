# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, potential clients, and fellow developers who land on fraimer.dev after
finding Fraimer through GitHub, X, Bluesky, Discord, or a shared project link. They
arrive on a phone or laptop, curious and skimming, deciding within seconds whether
this person is worth reaching out to. Three overlapping jobs they might be doing:

- **Hiring / commissioning** — sizing up whether to pay Fraimer for work (the terms
  page and Ko-fi links serve this path).
- **Orienting** — using the site as a hub to find the right social/contact channel.
- **Evaluating** — scanning projects and skills to judge credibility and range.

The site must serve all three at once without forcing a choice.

## Product Purpose

fraimer.dev is the personal home base and calling card of Fraimer De La Cruz — a
17-year-old programmer and CS student from the Dominican Republic working in
full-stack development and AI agents. It exists to make a strong first impression,
route people to the right channel, showcase real shipped projects, and open the door
to paid work. Success looks like: a visitor who came from a single link leaves
convinced Fraimer is a serious, ambitious builder — and knows exactly how to hire or
reach him.

## Brand Personality

**Bold, futuristic, ambitious** — with credibility that never slips. The voice is
confident and forward-looking, the voice of someone building the future and not
apologizing for aiming high. Personable and direct (first person, real emoji, "Hey
there you!"), but the ambition is always backed by shipped work, not just vibes.
Three words: **ambitious, precise, distinctive.** The site should feel like momentum.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dev-portfolio slop.** The interchangeable dark-mode developer template:
  grid overlay + floating dots + gradient orbs + mono headings. The current site
  leans into this and it must be broken out of — it reads as a template, not a person.
- **Corporate SaaS landing.** Sterile, safe, cream-and-blue startup page with no
  point of view.
- **Overdesigned / cluttered.** Bold must not mean noisy. Effects that compete with
  the content, or spectacle that buries the projects and contact paths, fail.
- **Childish / unprofessional.** Playfulness must never undercut the "hire me" signal;
  the site still has to close paid work.

## Design Principles

- **Ambition with credibility.** Every bold, future-facing choice must still read as
  "this person is worth paying." Wow and trust are not a trade-off here — earn both.
- **Signal over noise.** Bold is not cluttered. One strong, committed idea beats ten
  decorative effects. When in doubt, remove.
- **Earn the spectacle.** Motion and visual drama must serve the content — direct the
  eye to the work, the identity, or the contact path. Ambient effects that decorate
  without guiding are the slop being rejected.
- **Show the work.** The projects and shipped proof are the argument; identity and
  personality frame it. Make the real accomplishments (the hackathon win, live
  products) impossible to miss.
- **Distinctly Fraimer.** The site should be unmistakably his — not a theme anyone
  could have downloaded. Personal specifics (the DR, the age, the actual projects,
  the voice) are the differentiator, not generic "modern developer" signifiers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast (the current gray-400/500 text on
black needs auditing against this), large text ≥3:1, fully keyboard-navigable with
visible focus states, and semantic structure. All motion — and there is a lot of it
currently (grid, orbs, dots, lines) — must have a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`
fallback. Bold visuals should never come at the cost of legibility.
